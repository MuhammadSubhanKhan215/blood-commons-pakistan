The Blood Commons — Pakistan

Somewhere tonight, a father is dialling the seventh number on a list.

He is not looking for a miracle. He is looking for one bag of O-negative, which already exists, in a fridge, eleven minutes away. Nobody has told him where.

The Blood Commons exists so that call never has to be made again.

One of a hundred thousand

“Every twenty-one days I take my daughter to the ward and I start asking strangers. I have learned which of my cousins say yes, and which ones stop answering.”

— Mother of a thalassemia patient, Multan

More than a hundred thousand people in Pakistan live on other people's blood. Their families spend their lives building private networks of favours because no public one exists. We are building the public one.

What we believe

Blood should never be a favour

Today, four out of five bags come from a frightened relative in a hospital corridor. That is not a system. That is a family carrying a weight alone.

Distance is the real disease

The blood usually exists. It is twelve kilometres away, in a fridge nobody thought to call. We match by type, by distance, by how little time is left.

Trust is built in the open

Every bag can be followed from the arm it left to the arm it entered. Not for paperwork — so a mother can believe what she is told.

People come back when it means something

We tell donors who they helped, and when. In our pilot, 73 out of 100 came back. Gratitude keeps better than blood does.

“No one should die within eleven minutes of the thing that would have saved them.”
